Ethics & Positionality

How This Map
Takes a Position

This map does not claim to be neutral. Every cartographic decision — what to show, what to name, how to draw connections — carries interpretive weight. This page explains the commitments behind those decisions.

On Neutrality

Mapping is always an interpretive act. Mapping Indigenous land, Ancestors, and cultural relationships carries particular ethical weight that extends beyond technical choices. The framing here — arcs that originate from Tribal Nations, not institutions; language that names removal rather than describing it as "collection" — reflects a deliberate positioning.

This is not a database. It is an argument: that the geographic distance between where Ancestors currently reside and where they belong is meaningful, measurable, and unresolved.

On What the Data Represents

Connections shown between institutions and Tribal Nations document relationships established through archaeological, historical, and Tribal evidence as recorded in Federal Register notices (2020–2026). This evidence was produced within a legal system that itself has a complicated and evolving relationship with Indigenous sovereignty.

A Federal Register notice marks a moment in the repatriation process — not its conclusion. Many items documented here remain in institutional custody pending physical transfer, ongoing consultation, or unresolved jurisdictional questions. An arc on this map does not mean an ancestor has come home.

On the Author's Position

This project was created by Rylee LaLonde, a PhD student in Anthropology at Michigan State University and an enrolled member of the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians. That positionality shapes both what was built and how. Being a Tribal citizen does not resolve the ethical tensions involved in visualizing repatriation data — it complicates them in ways that are generative rather than paralyzing.

This work was developed in collaboration with Dr. Jessica Yann (MSU NAGPRA Program Manager) and Dr. Madeline Mackie (Assistant Professor, MSU Anthropology), whose expertise and guidance shaped the project's scope and framing.

Guiding Commitments

Transparency

All data sources are cited. All citations are disclosed on the Sources page. The methodology for notice compilation is documented and reviewable.

Tribal Sovereignty

Tribal Nations are named and treated as sovereign political entities — not ethnic categories, archaeological groupings, or heritage objects.

Avoiding Harm

This project does not display sensitive information about individual human remains, sacred objects, or burial site locations.

Ongoing Process

Repatriation is not presented as complete or resolved. The map reflects a snapshot of an ongoing, dynamic, and politically contested relationship.
